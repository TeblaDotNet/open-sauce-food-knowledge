## Existing draft improvements

### equipment: blender

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. A processor can leave deliberately coarse pieces or use a disc to slice and shred. A blender is usually chosen for a flowing, smooth mixture. Overlapping puree tasks do not make the appliances equivalent; use the attachment and batch size the recipe actually needs.

Evidence: [KitchenAid: Difference Between a Food Processor and Blender](https://www.kitchenaid.ca/en_ca/blog/s/food-processor-vs-blender.html). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: food-processor

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. A blade chops material already in the bowl, whereas a slicing disc cuts food as it enters. Choosing a disc instead of running the bowl blade changes the kind of result, not just its fineness. Blender recipes with plentiful liquid may need a different work-bowl limit.

Evidence: [KitchenAid: Difference Between a Food Processor and Blender](https://www.kitchenaid.ca/en_ca/blog/s/food-processor-vs-blender.html). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: stand-mixer

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Tilt-head and bowl-lift describe how the bowl and mixing head are accessed, not different culinary processes. Optional pasta rollers and grinders are separate attachments: having a stand mixer does not by itself provide those functions.

Evidence: [KitchenAid: Stand Mixer Buying Guide](https://www.kitchenaid.com/content/kitchenaid/en_us/countertop-appliances/stand-mixer-buying-guide.html). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: steaming-basket

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. The basket supports food above the water while allowing steam through; a lid and suitable pan complete the cooking arrangement. A colander can drain food, but that alone does not establish that it fits a covered pan or is suitable for heating.

Evidence: [Land O’Lakes: Kitchen Equipment and Their Uses](https://www.landolakes.com/kitchen-reference/equipment-guide/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: thermometer

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. The measurement location matters as much as the display. An appliance thermometer describes the surrounding cooking environment; a food probe describes the region around its sensing area. These readings answer different questions, so an oven reading does not replace a measurement within the food.

Evidence: [USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service: Food Thermometers](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-thermometers). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. USDA refetch returned 403; restricted to existing sourced project evidence.

### equipment: pressure-cooker

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. A pressure-canner reference describes equipment and a validated jar-processing system. A pressure-cooker recipe describes cooking a meal. Similar pressure-related names do not transfer one system’s evidence to the other; choose the applicable documentation before treating them as substitutes.

Evidence: [National Center for Home Food Preservation: How to Use Boiling Water and Pressure Canners](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/general-canning-information/recommended-canners/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: mandoline

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Thin, repeated vegetable slices are useful in fermented-vegetable preparation as well as salads. Slicing and pounding have different jobs: a slicer sets piece shape, while pounding or massaging releases juice. A uniform cut alone does not establish fermentation conditions.

Evidence: [Illinois Extension: Fermenting Equipment](https://extension.illinois.edu/food-preservation/fermenting-equipment). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: wok

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Stir-fry ingredients are often added in stages because they need different cooking times. A wok provides space for that sequence, but the vessel name does not make a crowded mixture a stir-fry. Match the preparation and loading to the intended method.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: The Essential Cooking Techniques](https://www.ciafoodies.com/the-essential-cooking-techniques/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: dutch-oven

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. The covered stage of a braise retains moisture; reducing its cooking liquid is a separate, evaporative step. A Dutch oven can participate in both, but keeping the same lid position throughout does not serve both purposes.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: The Essential Cooking Techniques](https://www.ciafoodies.com/the-essential-cooking-techniques/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### equipment: cast-iron-frying-pan

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Skillet describes the shallow cooking form; cast iron describes the material. A cast-iron Dutch oven is a deeper, lidded vessel. Shared material does not make their capacity, access for turning food or evaporation behaviour identical.

Evidence: [Lodge: Cast Iron Product Guide](https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/lodge-cast-iron-product-guide-1). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: simmer

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. A simmer describes the liquid’s activity, while reduction describes losing liquid through evaporation. A covered simmer can cook food without the same reduction as an uncovered pan. State the desired consistency separately when a sauce must also concentrate.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: Lid or No Lid?](https://www.ciafoodies.com/lid-or-no-lid/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: boil

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Boiling food immerses it in hot liquid; steaming exposes it to vapour above liquid. These are different heat-transfer arrangements even when both begin with boiling water. A steamer basket therefore does not make its contents boiled food.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: Cooking Principles: Poaching, Steaming, Boiling and Simmering](https://www.ciaconsulting.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Cooking-principles-I-Day-4-Poaching-Steaming-En-Papillote-Boiling-Simmering-CP.pdf). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: roast

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Covering a roasting vessel changes how moisture escapes and introduces a trapped-steam environment. Browning an exposed surface and retaining moisture under a lid are different aims; a recipe may deliberately move between them.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: Lid or No Lid?](https://www.ciafoodies.com/lid-or-no-lid/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: braise

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. A covered cooking stage and an uncovered sauce-finishing stage can belong to the same dish. Removing the lid encourages evaporation; this explains why the sauce may be reduced separately after the food is tender.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: Lid or No Lid?](https://www.ciafoodies.com/lid-or-no-lid/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: knead

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Kneading develops a dough’s structure; pastry lamination preserves distinct layers of dough and fat through rolling and folding. Both involve working dough, but continuous mixing is not a substitute for the layer-building operation.

Evidence: [King Arthur Baking: How we make puff pastry at the King Arthur bakery](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2013/08/02/puff-pastry-layer-it-like-the-pros). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: reduce

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Adding a thickener and evaporating water can both change consistency, but they do not do the same work. Reduction removes liquid rather than adding body to it. A recipe’s target volume and its texture cue should therefore be read together.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: Lid or No Lid?](https://www.ciafoodies.com/lid-or-no-lid/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: poach

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Deep poaching fully surrounds the food with cooking liquid. The liquid can contribute flavour through stock, aromatics or wine, while a serving sauce may be prepared separately. Poaching does not require turning all of the cooking liquid into the final sauce.

Evidence: [Culinary Institute of America: Deep Poaching: A Healthy Technique for Delicious Foods](https://www.ciafoodies.com/deep-poaching-a-healthy-technique-for-delicious-foods/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: score

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Surface scoring and dough docking both make openings, but docking uses small punctures to limit puffing. Do not treat a decorative or expansion-guiding slash as equivalent to pricking the dough across its surface.

Evidence: [King Arthur Baking: Shortbread Cookies](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/shortbread-cookies-recipe). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: blind-bake

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Docking controls puffing by making small holes; blind-baking cooks an empty pastry case. They can occur together, but one is a preparation and the other a baking stage. Docking a crust does not mean that it has already been blind-baked.

Evidence: [King Arthur Baking: Shortbread Cookies](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/shortbread-cookies-recipe). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.

### process: ferment

Improved existing draft; no earlier content removed. Fermentation names the microbial transformation, not a promise that living organisms remain in the finished food. Later processing can change that. The process name alone does not establish a product’s storage conditions or a health benefit.

Evidence: [ISAPP / Maria Marco: Creating a scientific definition of fermented foods](https://isappscience.org/creating-a-scientific-definition-of-fermented-foods/). Status: 0.1 draft/generated. Editorial comparison; no universal operating limits inferred.
