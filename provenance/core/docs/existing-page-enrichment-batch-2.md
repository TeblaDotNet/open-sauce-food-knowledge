### equipment: plate

Priority: Most-used remaining equipment; explain serving shape. Corpus occurrences: 58.

A shallow piece of tableware for presenting individual portions. Side plates suit smaller servings or sandwiches, while deeper plate forms can hold saucier foods.

Consider the usable surface and depth, not just the outside diameter. A broad rim reduces the central serving area, and a flat plate contains liquid differently from a soup plate. Materials and sizes vary; plate alone does not specify bakeware.

Sources: [Salad & side plates](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/cat/side-plates-18863/); [Deep plates](https://www.ikea.com/ch/en/cat/deep-plates-18862/).

Qualification: No universal dimensions, material or oven suitability inferred. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### equipment: dish

Priority: Common broad container word; avoid conflating serving and baking. Corpus occurrences: 14.

A broad term for a vessel used to hold, prepare or present food. A baking dish is one particular form: its sides contain layered mixtures and liquids during oven cooking.

Some purpose-made baking dishes also serve for marinating, breading or taking a bake to the table. Those capabilities belong to the particular vessel. The unqualified word dish does not tell you its depth, capacity or compatibility with direct heat.

Sources: [Baking Dishes](https://www.lecreuset.ca/en_CA/bakeware/baking-dishes).

Qualification: Generic dish remains broader than a baking dish or platter. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### equipment: jar

Priority: Frequently used container with mouth and closure distinctions. Corpus occurrences: 14.

A container with an opening that can be closed with a lid. The mouth affects access: wide-mouth canning jars are easier to fill and empty than narrower examples.

Closure design matters independently of capacity. A screw band, lid and sealing surface work together on a purpose-made canning jar; an ordinary storage jar is not evidence of suitability for a heat-processing recipe.

Sources: [Recommended Jars and Lids](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/general-canning-information/recommended-jars-and-lids/).

Qualification: No canning method or storage life supplied. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### equipment: cloth

Priority: Preparation material with useful handling example. Corpus occurrences: 6.

A flexible fabric used as a preparation aid. In strudel making, a cloth spread over the work area supports the thin dough and can help lift and roll it around the filling.

This use relies on a broad flexible support rather than a rigid board. Other food-contact tasks require different fabric properties: a cloth used to guide dough is not automatically a fine straining cloth. Choose it for the operation described.

Sources: [Homemade Strudel](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2017/01/19/homemade-strudel).

Qualification: No equivalence with cheesecloth or universal fabric suitability proposed. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### equipment: baking-mold

Priority: Useful distinction between shaping and merely containing. Corpus occurrences: 4.

A baking vessel whose cavity gives the bake its form. A shaped cake pan illustrates how the finished surface follows the details of the mold.

Release is part of the preparation: grease or otherwise prepare the cavity as directed for that recipe and material. Intricate shapes need attention to recesses, and batter needs room to expand. Do not assume a non-stick surface removes every preparation step.

Sources: [Bundt Cake](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/bundt-cake).

Qualification: No fixed fill level or release procedure generalized to all molds. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### equipment: squeeze-bottle

A squeezable container that dispenses liquid through a tip, allowing one-handed application. Typical uses include adding oil to a pan or drawing sauce lines over a finished dish.

The tip controls the stream; a fine tip favours a narrow drizzle. The cited bottle has an attached cap, but closures and materials vary. Dispensing convenience does not establish suitability for heating the bottle or for long-term storage.

### equipment: gridiron

Priority: Historical Corpus tool benefits from a concrete structural distinction. Corpus occurrences: 2.

An open framework of metal bars used to support food over heat. Historical hearth examples held meat or fish above hot coals.

Gaps between the bars distinguish it from a solid griddle surface. Food rests on the bars rather than on a continuous plate; a museum example documents this construction without making every gridiron a complete barbecue appliance.

Sources: [Gridiron (dated 1708)](https://www.manuscriptcookbookssurvey.org/collection/Detail/utensils/1162).

### equipment: glass-jars

### process: refrigerate

Priority: Most-used remaining process; distinguish controlled cold storage. Corpus occurrences: 30.

Place food in a refrigerator to keep it chilled. Refrigeration slows bacterial growth; it is a storage condition rather than a cooking or freezing step.

Cold air needs room to circulate, so avoid tightly overpacking the appliance. Covered containers help keep stored foods separated, and an appliance thermometer checks the actual temperature. Follow food-specific storage guidance: the verb refrigerate alone does not specify a safe storage life.

Sources: [Refrigerator Thermometers: Cold Facts about Food Safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/refrigerator-thermometers-cold-facts-about-food-safety).

Qualification: No universal shelf life or preservation guarantee. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: press

Priority: Frequent broad mechanical action with distinct purposes. Corpus occurrences: 17.

Apply pressure to food or a mixture for a stated result. Pressing shortbread dough into a pan shapes an even layer; pressing cut tomatoes can release pulp and seeds.

The object and direction determine the task. Shaping dough, attaching a coating and forcing food through a sieve are not interchangeable instructions. Use the recipe's endpoint rather than treating press as a fixed force or a request to puree everything.

### process: turn

Change which side of a piece of food faces the heat or cooking surface. In frying, turning exposes another surface for cooking and browning.

Use a suitable utensil to move the piece without unnecessarily piercing its crust. How often to turn depends on the method; the handling appropriate to pan-frying is not automatically the same as for food immersed in fat.

Sources: [Tips for Frying on the Stove](https://www.ciafoodies.com/tips-for-frying-on-the-stove/).

### process: wash

Priority: Frequent instruction needs ingredient-specific treatment. Corpus occurrences: 16.

Clean the surface of food using an appropriate washing method. For fresh produce, FDA guidance uses running water; firm produce can also be rubbed or scrubbed with a clean produce brush.

Produce washing is not sterilization, and soap or detergent is not recommended on fruits and vegetables. Ready-to-eat produce labelled as pre-washed can be used without another wash. These produce instructions should not be generalized to every food.

Sources: [Selecting and Serving Produce Safely](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely).

Qualification: Produce-scoped example; no instruction to wash raw meat or poultry. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: seal

Priority: Common action with pastry and container contexts. Corpus occurrences: 9.

Close an opening or join edges so the contents are held in place. In a hand pie, pressing the pastry edges together with a fork helps keep filling from escaping at the seam.

### process: separate

Divide components so they can be handled independently. Separating an egg keeps the yolk apart from the white, allowing each to be used where the recipe needs it.

An egg separator lets the white pass through while retaining the yolk. Elsewhere the recipe may ask to separate cloves or pieces of food; those are different physical tasks. Read what must be separated rather than assuming the verb always means egg separation.

Sources: [Important Food Safety Information](https://www.incredibleegg.org/egg-handling-storage-tips/).

### process: wrap

Priority: Common enclosing action; distinguish storage wrapper from cooking parcel. Corpus occurrences: 9.

Enclose food or a preparation in a flexible covering. Pastry disks may be wrapped before chilling so they can rest as individual prepared pieces.

The material must suit the next step. A wrapper used while chilling is not automatically suitable for baking or other heating. Wrapping also differs from simply laying a loose cover over a bowl; follow the recipe's instruction about what is enclosed.

Sources: [Pie crust](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/pie-crust).

Qualification: No inference that storage wrap is heat-safe or airtight. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: pat-dry

Priority: Surface preparation distinct from thorough dehydration. Corpus occurrences: 4.

Blot surface moisture with a clean absorbent cloth or paper towel. After washing produce, drying removes water remaining on the outside.

Gentle contact targets surface droplets rather than squeezing liquid out of the food or dehydrating it throughout. FDA produce guidance specifies a clean cloth or paper towel; reusing a soiled cloth defeats that handling step.

Sources: [Selecting and Serving Produce Safely](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely).

Qualification: Produce example only; no preservation or shelf-stability implication. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: slow-cook

Priority: Useful heat-method distinction backed by appliance guidance. Corpus occurrences: 4.

Cook with gentle heat over an extended period. A covered electric slow cooker is one implementation, combining heat from the pot with moisture and steam inside the vessel.

High and low settings change the cooking rate, and lifting the lid loses heat. Use a recipe and loading guidance suited to the appliance; the general instruction slow-cook does not justify an arbitrary low setting or a universal duration for every food.

Sources: [Slow Cookers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/slow-cookers-and-food-safety).

Qualification: Electric-cooker example is not an exclusive definition; no universal safety schedule. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: skin

Priority: Ingredient-specific removal task; distinguish fish scales. Corpus occurrences: 3.

Remove the skin of an ingredient. For tomatoes, brief exposure to hot water followed by rapid cooling can loosen the skin so it can be peeled away.

The method depends on the food: removing skin from a fish is different from loosening tomato skin. Descaling removes scales from a fish surface and is a separate operation; skin does not prescribe one knife technique for every ingredient.

Sources: [Monder](https://chefsimon.com/articles/lexique-monder); [Techniques appliquees a la preparation des poissons](https://chefsimon.com/articles/sommaire-techniques-appliquees-a-la-preparation-des-poissons).

Qualification: Tomato example does not generalize to all nuts, fish or poultry. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: descale

Priority: Low-frequency but clear fish-preparation distinction. Corpus occurrences: 1.

Remove scales from the surface of a fish using a scaler or an appropriate knife action. The scales are detached from the skin rather than the whole skin being removed.

Descaling is one preparatory task among others such as trimming fins or gutting. Whether it is needed depends on the species and intended preparation; it should not be treated as a synonym for filleting or skinning.

Sources: [Ecailler un poisson portion](https://chefsimon.com/gourmets/chef-simon/recettes/ecailler-un-poisson-portion).

Qualification: Species-dependent; no freshness or safety test inferred from appearance. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: temper-spices

Priority: Zero literal uses but useful existing culturally scoped method. Corpus occurrences: 0.

Heat spices in hot oil or ghee so their flavour passes into the fat. In Indian cooking, the flavoured fat may start a dish or be added near the end as a finishing step.

The sequence matters because different spices and aromatics cook at different rates. This is distinct from dry-roasting spices, and from tempering chocolate or eggs. Tadka is one relevant culinary context, not a universal replacement name for every practice involving spices and fat.

Sources: [Cinnamon Kitchen: The Cookbook — Tempering (p. 27)](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781472934932_A29775035/preview-9781472934932_A29775035.pdf).

Qualification: Regional practices vary; no new aliases or fixed time/temperature instructions. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: shock

Priority: Useful distinct cooling step already eligible for a page. Corpus occurrences: 0.

Cool food rapidly, often immediately after a hot-water or steam treatment. Plunging blanched vegetables into cold or ice water interrupts continued cooking.

Blanching supplies the heat; shocking supplies the rapid cooling. They can be consecutive steps without being the same action. In the cited vegetable-freezing guidance, the vegetables are then drained thoroughly; chilling slowly in a refrigerator is a different cooling approach.

Sources: [Blanching Vegetables](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/freeze-general-information/blanching-vegetables/).

Qualification: Vegetable example; no full freezing or preservation procedure supplied. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: butter

Priority: Common instruction whose object changes its purpose. Corpus occurrences: 9.

Apply or incorporate butter as the preparation requires. Buttering a mold coats its surface to help release the bake; buttering bread spreads butter onto the food itself.

The object matters. Adding butter to a sauce is different from coating a pan, and a buttered mold may still need flour or another lining. Follow the specified preparation rather than assuming butter alone makes every bake release cleanly.

Sources: [Beurrer](https://chefsimon.com/articles/lexique-beurrer).

Qualification: No universal substitution or release guarantee. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.

### process: flour

Priority: Useful surface-coating action distinct from adding flour to a mixture. Corpus occurrences: 3.

Coat or dust a surface with flour. On a buttered baking mold, moving the mold spreads flour over the base and sides; tapping removes the excess.

On a pastry work surface, a light dusting helps control sticking during rolling. Both uses put flour on a surface rather than treating it as a measured ingredient to mix in. The amount and surface preparation depend on the recipe.

Sources: [Chemiser un moule](https://chefsimon.com/gourmets/chef-simon/recettes/chemiser-un-moule); [Pie crust](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/pie-crust).

Qualification: No fixed flour quantity or universal pan-preparation rule. Review: provisional 0.1; no human review.
